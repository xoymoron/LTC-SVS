Adding Local Technique Control to a Pretrained Singing Voice Synthesis Model

Minjun Kim · Yonsei University

Abstract

Can a singing voice model add vibrato, falsetto, or breathiness to a few words while preserving the rest of a performance? We adapt SoulX-Singer to accept a technique label on each word. We train the label embeddings and score encoder on 5,415 English and Mandarin clips from GTSinger while keeping the flow decoder frozen. During synthesis, a guidance scale controls how strongly the model follows an ON or OFF request. We evaluated edits in 3,310 target regions from two held-out singers. At the strongest guidance, vibrato ON increased pitch modulation on average. Breathiness ON and OFF both lowered the harmonics-to-noise ratio, showing that the two commands did not produce opposite acoustic changes.

Measurement notes

The technique probe scores the target region. The matched-note vibrato measure uses a sustained note measurable at every guidance setting for that model. For add requests, the valid window can differ across models. WavLM compares the full phrase with a reference from another song by the same singer. Changes in lyric error rates are in percentage points. A dash indicates an unavailable measurement.

All spectrograms for a phrase share a scale relative to their peak mel power. Each uses 80 mel bands, a 1,024-sample Hann window and a 10 ms hop at 24 kHz.
